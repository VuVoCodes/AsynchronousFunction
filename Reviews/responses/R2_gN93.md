# Response to Reviewer gN93 (Borderline Accept, 4)

We thank the reviewer for a careful review whose four concerns all target the right places. We answer each with new measurements or concrete text commitments.

## Q1: What do the safety properties say in the high-imbalance regime?

The descent bound (Prop. 3) is **uniform in the imbalance level**: it holds for any utilization gap, so in high-imbalance training the guarantee is that PGGB cannot cause divergence and inflates gradient variance by at most s_max^2 = 4 in the worst case. Prop. 2 (self-attenuation) is the complementary low-imbalance guarantee: the intervention vanishes as the gap closes. What is deliberately **not** guaranteed is that boosting closes the gap in high imbalance; that claim is empirical (Section 4.4: weak probe +9.41 pp, strong probe -0.81 pp, post-hoc gap reduced 5.4x). As diagnostic criteria for when boosting may become harmful, our operating-conditions analysis (Section 4.3.1) identifies the failure regime observed in practice (thin data budget combined with weak inherent asymmetry, where the failure localizes to the throttling component rather than the boost), and the monitored quantities delta and s_bar are themselves the online warning signal: practitioners can log them at no extra cost. We will state this scoping explicitly in Section 3.5 and expand the Limitations accordingly.

## Q2: How is the dataset-level delta computed, and why threshold 0.15?

Delta is the final-epoch EMA-smoothed probe-accuracy gap measured during **baseline** training (no intervention), averaged over 5 seeds (Appendix B.9, Table 9). We will state this definition in Section 4.1 as requested. On why 0.15: the measured values separate into a low group (AVE 0.017, KS 0.038, Sarcasm 0.076, Twitter15 0.150) and a high group (CMU-MOSI 0.222, CREMA-D 0.268, with the CMU-MOSEI value under re-measurement on re-verified data), leaving an empty interval between 0.150 and 0.222. Under the paper's rule (high imbalance if delta exceeds the threshold), every threshold in [0.150, 0.222) produces the identical categorization, so 0.15 is not a tuned quantity: it is a round value inside this gap, and since delta is measured on baseline training alone, the categorization is fixed independently of any method's results. Twitter15 (0.150 +/- 0.096) is the boundary case with the largest seed variance, and we will note this explicitly.

## Q3: Unimodal analysis on the text-dominant sentiment benchmarks

We ran the requested analysis: post-hoc per-modality linear probes on the saved final checkpoints of all sentiment runs, mirroring the Appendix B.8 protocol (mean over 5 seeds). 

**CMU-MOSEI** (probe accuracy %, baseline → PGGB boost-only): text 64.95 → 66.43, audio 51.29 → 53.17, vision 59.78 → 60.09. PGGB does improve the weaker audio representation (+1.9 pp) and vision slightly (+0.3 pp), but text also rises (+1.5 pp), and OGM-GE shows the same all-modality pattern (+2.7/+2.9/+1.6). The utilization gap barely moves (13.65 → 13.26 baseline vs. PGGB, 13.04 for the composition). The interventions act more like regularizers of a data-starved optimization than like targeted rebalancers.

**CMU-MOSI**: text 70.99 → 71.72, audio 48.86 → 49.24, vision 54.49 → 53.47. No movement exceeds 1 pp, within seed noise, and the gap is static (~22 pp).

The contrast with CREMA-D (App. B.8: weak visual probe +9.41 pp, strong audio probe −0.81 pp, gap reduced 5.4x) is the direct answer to the reviewer's question: targeted weak-modality recovery occurs where the weak modality is undertrained (trainable encoders, SGD, severe imbalance), whereas on the text-dominant frozen-feature pipelines PGGB self-attenuates rather than forcing uninformative signal — the behavior Prop. 2 is designed to give. Adam additionally absorbs roughly two-thirds of the applied boost in these pipelines (update-norm measurement in the global response). We will add the full probe tables to the appendix and fold the undertrained-versus-intrinsically-limited distinction into Section 5 and the Limitations.

## Q4: Would increasing alpha help on text-dominant tasks?

Neither helps nor degrades, in every setting tested. We ran the suggested alpha-sensitivity study (boost-only, alpha in {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5}, seed 42) on all two sentiment pipelines:

| Dataset | 0.25 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1.0 | 1.5 | Range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CMU-MOSI | 73.32 | 72.74 | 72.89 | 73.03 | 72.59 | 0.73 pp |
| CMU-MOSEI | 70.24 | 70.24 | 70.68 | 71.33 | 70.68 | 1.09 pp |

On CMU-MOSI, the high-imbalance case, the grid spans 0.73 pp with no monotone trend, entirely within the ~0.3–0.8 pp seed-noise floor of these pipelines. CMU-MOSEI is mildly positive to flat, with a +1.1 pp bump at alpha=1.0 that sits at the edge of the noise floor. Crucially, accuracy does not degrade at any alpha up to 1.5 on any of the two benchmarks, so we find no evidence that stronger boosting amplifies noisy, less informative modalities into accuracy loss. This is consistent with the two mechanisms already quantified: the bounded scaling s <= s_max caps the intervention, and Adam's second-moment normalization absorbs roughly two-thirds of what remains. Conversely, raising alpha also cannot unlock additional audio/visual learning here, because (per Q3) those representations are intrinsically limited rather than suppressed — there is little recoverable signal for the boost to transmit. We will add the sweep (extended to multiple seeds) to the appendix and state this scoping in the Limitations.

## Limitations

We will expand the Limitations paragraph to cover the three requested items: scoping of the safety guarantees in high imbalance, sensitivity of the imbalance categorization (boundary case Twitter15), and the distinction between undertrained and intrinsically less informative modalities, which PGGB does not attempt to distinguish.
